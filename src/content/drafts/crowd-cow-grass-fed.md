Crowd Cow sells beef from several farms and ranches, and only part of it carries the company's "100% Grass Fed" badge. On November 6, 2026, most of that grass-fed range came from a Uruguayan supplier, with a few items from US producers in Nebraska, Wisconsin and Minnesota. Grass-fed ground beef and burger patties cost about $15 a pound, steaks ran roughly $42 to $114 a pound, and shipping was free on orders of $149 or more.

The catch is that Crowd Cow also sells "Pasture Raised" beef, which by its own description is finished on grain. The two badges sit side by side, sometimes on the same cut from the same supplier, so you need to check the badge on every item.

## How we put this review together

Everything below comes from Crowd Cow's own website, read on November 6, 2026: its grass-fed beef collection, its main beef page and FAQ, a ranch page, its help center, its subscriptions page and two older company posts. We did not order, cook or taste any Crowd Cow product. Several farm websites could not be reached when we checked, so supplier details here are Crowd Cow's descriptions, not the farms' own. Prices change often and the site was running holiday promotions, so treat every number as a snapshot and check the current price before you order.

## What Crowd Cow means by "100% grass-fed"

Crowd Cow sorts its beef into categories. Two matter here:

- **100% Grass Fed.** The grass-fed collection page describes grass-finished cattle as raised on mother's milk and forage only. Forage means grass, hay and other plants the animal grazes or is fed, not grain. The main beef page describes this line as both 100% grass-fed and grass-finished.
- **Pasture Raised.** The main beef page says these animals graze on grass but are finished on grain. In other words, Crowd Cow's pasture-raised beef is grain-finished. "Pasture-raised" describes where an animal spent time, not what it ate at the end.

That distinction is the single most useful thing to know before you shop. Our guide to [grass-fed vs grass-finished](/guides/grass-fed-vs-grass-finished/) explains why the finishing diet matters and what the USDA label rules do and don't require.

Two more points on the wording:

- Crowd Cow's "100% Grass Fed" badge is the company's own label. On the pages we read, no grass-fed product named a third-party grass-fed certifier, such as the American Grassfed Association or A Greener World. Our [certifications page](/certifications/) lists the programs that actually check what cattle eat.
- The badge is not always shown the same way. A Certified Piedmontese New York strip had the "100% Grass Fed" badge on the main beef page but only a sale badge on the grass-fed collection page. And Frigorifico Pando's ribeye, New York strip and filet mignon each appeared in two listings: one tagged "100% Grass Fed" and one tagged "Pasture Raised," at slightly different prices. Read the badge on the exact listing you add to your cart.

Crowd Cow's FAQ also says every product on the site is raised without "unnecessary antibiotics" or any added hormones. "Unnecessary" leaves room for treating sick animals, so if a strict no-antibiotics standard matters to you, ask the company what it means for the product you want.

## Where the grass-fed beef comes from

Crowd Cow says it works directly with independent farms rather than meat brokers. A 2019 company post says it put the actual farm's name on everything it sells, and current listings do show a producer name and location on each item. That is more than many delivery services give you.

On November 6, 2026, the items tagged "100% Grass Fed" on the pages we read came from four suppliers:

| Supplier as listed | Location as listed | Grass-fed items seen |
|---|---|---|
| Frigorifico Pando Grass Fed Beef | Uruguay | Filet mignon, ribeye, ribeye pair, tri-tip, burger patties, stir fry |
| Certified Piedmontese | Lincoln, Nebraska | Ground beef, tenderloin medallions, New York strip |
| Pederson's Natural Beef Farms | Wisconsin | Hot dogs |
| Thousand Hills Lifetime Grazed | Minnesota | Pork and beef bratwurst (sold out) |

A few things stand out:

- **Most of the range is imported.** Six of the grass-fed beef items were from Uruguay. Crowd Cow's collection page describes these cattle as raised on Uruguay's pampas, its wide natural grasslands. "Frigorífico" is Spanish for a meat-packing plant, so the name points to a processor rather than one ranch. If you want to know the farm behind it, ask Crowd Cow.
- **US grass-fed choices were limited.** Only a handful of US items carried the grass-fed badge, and one was sold out. Crowd Cow's best-known US ranch on the beef page, Little Belt Cattle Co. in Martinsdale, Montana, was tagged "Pasture Raised" on every product. Its ranch page describes holistic and regenerative grazing but does not say the cattle are grass-finished.
- **Mixed products.** The Thousand Hills bratwurst is pork and beef, so read ingredient lists on sausages and hot dogs if you want beef only.

Origin also matters for labels. Since January 1, 2026, FSIS (the USDA agency that regulates meat labels) allows a "Product of USA" or "Made in the USA" claim only if the animal was born, raised, slaughtered and processed in the US. Uruguayan beef does not qualify. The claim is voluntary, though, so a US product without it is not necessarily imported.

Crowd Cow has sourced from abroad for years. Its October 2018 press release announced grass-fed beef from Tasmania, Australia, along with Wagyu (a Japanese cattle breed known for heavy marbling) from Japan and beef from Spain, Argentina and Italy. Most of those were not on the grass-fed pages in November 2026, which is a reminder that the range changes.

What this means for your budget:

- **Ground beef is the value item, but not cheap.** At $14.99 a pound, Crowd Cow's grass-fed ground beef tied for the highest list price in our comparison of [grass-fed beef delivery services](/guides/best-grass-fed-beef-delivery/), where the cheapest of six services was $8.00 a pound.

- **Promotions change the math.** On November 6, site banners offered 15% off orders of $199 or more with a holiday code, plus other limited offers. We did not verify the terms, so compare prices without discounts first, then apply any code at checkout.

## Shipping and subscriptions

According to Crowd Cow's help center and beef page FAQ:

- **Shipping cost.** Standard shipping is free on orders of $149 or more and a flat $19.99 below that. Priority (next-day) shipping costs $39.99 on top of standard, for orders under 20 pounds placed by 10 a.m. local time, Monday through Thursday.
- **Where it ships.** The beef page says Crowd Cow ships everywhere in the US except Alaska and Hawaii. The help center says standard delivery reaches most ZIP codes in the lower 48 states in one to two business days after payment.
- **Packaging.** Meat ships frozen with dry ice. The help center notes that the dry ice may be gone when the box arrives, and that the meat is still fine at 40°F or below on a food thermometer.
- **Changing an order.** You can cancel or reschedule any order that has not shipped yet from your order page.

You do not need a subscription to buy. If you do subscribe, the subscriptions page says:

- Boxes come every two, four, six or eight weeks.
- There is no membership fee.
- Subscribers get the lowest listed price on each cut, free shipping over $149 and extra reward points.
- You can change the contents, skip or pause, and reschedule without penalty until packing starts. Crowd Cow says it notifies you days before your box is packed.
- The box is built for you from your preferences and what is in stock.

The subscriptions page did not spell out how to cancel a subscription entirely, so check that before you sign up. Because subscription boxes are built partly from inventory, also confirm that every item in your box carries the "100% Grass Fed" badge, not "Pasture Raised."

## How much $149 buys

Reaching the $149 free-shipping line takes more meat than you might expect at these prices. By our math, $149 buys about 10 pounds of grass-fed ground beef at $14.99 a pound, or about 3.5 pounds of ribeye at $42.49 a pound. Most orders will be a mix. If you are weighing a bigger purchase, our guide to [quarter vs half vs whole cow](/guides/quarter-vs-half-vs-whole-cow/) covers how much meat each share gives and how much freezer space it needs.

## Who it suits, and who it doesn't

Crowd Cow's grass-fed range may suit you if:

- You want to buy grass-fed beef by the cut with no subscription required.
- You like seeing a producer name and location on each item.
- You are fine with beef from Uruguay, or you stick to the few US listings.
- You will order $149 or more at a time, so shipping is free.

Look elsewhere if:

- You want US-raised, grass-finished beef across many cuts. The US grass-fed selection was small when we looked.
- You want an independent grass-fed certification on the product.
- You want the lowest price per pound. A farm share or a lower-cost delivery service may cost less.

## Buying from a farm instead

If you would rather buy grass-finished beef straight from a US ranch, browse [our farm directory](/farms/), check our [bulk beef pages](/bulk-beef/) for shares in your state, and use our list of [questions to ask a farm](/guides/how-to-vet-a-grass-fed-farm/) before you pay a deposit. ## The bottom line

Crowd Cow is a marketplace, not a single grass-fed brand. Its "100% Grass Fed" badge, which it describes as grass-finished, covered mostly Uruguayan beef plus a few US products on November 6, 2026, while its "Pasture Raised" beef is grain-finished. Grass-fed ground beef was about $15 a pound and steaks much more, with free shipping at $149. Check the badge and origin on every listing, and confirm current prices on Crowd Cow's site before you order.
