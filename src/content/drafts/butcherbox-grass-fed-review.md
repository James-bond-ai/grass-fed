ButcherBox's 100% grass-fed beef comes from Australia, is sold mostly through a subscription box with free shipping, and on November 4, 2026, cost about $8 a pound for ground beef and about $31 a pound for ribeyes on its own website. Its bulk Quarter-Cow and Half-Cow boxes worked out to roughly $9 to $10 a pound by our count of the listed packs. Whether it suits you depends on whether imported beef, a subscription and the company's own feeding wording meet your standards.

This review covers only the 100% grass-fed line. ButcherBox also sells grain-finished beef from the US, which is a different product and is not covered here.

## How we put this review together

Everything below comes from ButcherBox's own website and help center, read on November 4, 2026, plus the public sources listed at the end of the page. We did not order, cook or taste any ButcherBox product. Prices and offers change often, so treat the numbers as a snapshot and check the current price before you order.

## What "100% grass-fed" means at ButcherBox

ButcherBox's help center says its "pasture-raised, 100% grass-fed beef" comes from Australia. Its FAQ says these cattle eat grass and forage on pasture for their whole lives and are never kept in a feedlot (a pen where cattle are fattened, usually on grain). One FAQ sentence also calls this beef "grass-finished," meaning the cattle stayed on grass and forage right up to slaughter.

The wording is not the same on every page, though:

- The FAQ and the Half-Cow Box page use "grass-finished."
- The 1 pound and 2 pound ground beef pages, and the ribeye page, say "100% grass-fed" but do not use "grass-finished."
- The Quarter-Cow Box page says "100% Grass-Fed" and describes a grass and forage diet for the animal's whole life.

In practice, ButcherBox describes the same Australian grass-fed line everywhere, and its whole-life grass and forage description is what you would want to see. If the word "grass-finished" on the package matters to you, read each product page, because not every one uses it. Our guide to [grass-fed vs grass-finished](/guides/grass-fed-vs-grass-finished/) explains why the difference matters.

The company also sells grain-finished beef. Its help center says those US cattle graze on pasture but that their diet adds hay, silage and grain near the end of life. ButcherBox says every product is labeled either 100% grass-fed or grain-finished. On its product list, grass-fed cuts carry a "100% Grass-Fed, Pasture-Raised" tag and the grain-finished cuts say "Pasture-Raised, Grain-Finished." Check the tag on every item you add.

## Where the beef comes from

ButcherBox says it sources beef from the US, Australia and Canada, and that its 100% grass-fed beef is from Australia. The Half-Cow Box page goes further: animals are born, raised and harvested in Australia, and the meat is cut and packaged in the US. The 1 pound ground beef page says "Product of Australia."

That matters for US labels. Since January 1, 2026, FSIS (the USDA agency that regulates meat labels) allows a "Product of USA" or "Made in the USA" claim on meat only if the animal was born, raised, slaughtered and processed in the US. Beef raised in Australia and cut in the US does not qualify. If you want US-raised grass-finished beef, ButcherBox's grass-fed line is not it; see [buying straight from a farm](#buying-from-a-farm-instead) below.

## Certifications and what they cover

ButcherBox says all its beef is certified pasture-raised and that all its land-based suppliers are audited and certified by Certified Humane or Global Animal Partnership (GAP). Both are animal welfare programs. They check how animals are housed and handled, and neither is a grass-fed feeding standard on its own. The help center does not say which of the two certifies the Australian grass-fed beef, and the 1 pound ground beef page shows only an "Animal Welfare Certification" note without naming a certifier. Its home page also shows a Certified B Corporation badge, which certifies the company as a whole, not the beef.

The Half-Cow Box page describes its beef as "certified organic," but the other grass-fed product pages we read did not, and none of them named an organic certifier. If organic matters to you, ask ButcherBox which certifier covers the specific product. Our [certifications page](/certifications/) and our guide to [grass-fed certifications](/guides/grass-fed-certifications-compared/) explain which programs actually check what cattle eat.

ButcherBox also says its meat is raised with no antibiotics or added hormones.

## What it costs

ButcherBox's main product is a subscription box. Its FAQ listed three sizes on November 4, 2026, with you choosing the cuts:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Medium | $179 | 6 | 21 lb | $8.52 |
| Large | $249 | 9 | 31 lb | $8.03 |
| Extra-Large | $319 | 12 | 42 lb | $7.60 |

The last column is our own math using the "up to" weight. You only get that weight if you pick heavier items such as ground beef and roasts. A box full of steaks weighs less and costs more per pound. ButcherBox's home page gives its own range of $8.52 to $15.23 a pound for the Medium box (effective January 8, 2026).

Neither bundle page states a total weight. We added up the listed packs (for example, eight 2 pound packs of ground beef in the Quarter-Cow Box) to get the weights above, using the 1 pound size shown on the product list for one item the bundle page did not weigh. Some cuts are listed as approximate, so your box may differ by a few pounds. Promotions and coupon codes do not apply to either bundle.

For context, the most common way to buy a quarter is straight from a farm. Using USDA's direct-to-consumer averages from September 2025, our [quarter cow guide](/guides/how-to-buy-a-quarter-cow/) estimates about $11 a pound of take-home meat for a grass-finished quarter. Those USDA averages are general direct-to-consumer prices, not grass-fed prices, and the number varies a lot by region and farm. A farm quarter also tends to be bigger: often 100 pounds or more. So ButcherBox's bundle price is in a similar range, but you are buying a fixed mix of cuts from imported cattle, not a share of one animal cut to your instructions.

## How the subscription works

- **Frequency.** Most members get a box every 4 weeks, according to the FAQ, but you can choose every 2 or 6 weeks and change your next bill date.
- **Pausing or canceling.** The FAQ says you can pause or cancel at any time from your Member Account page. An order already being processed still ships.
- **Shipping.** ButcherBox lists shipping as free on all boxes. Meat ships frozen with dry ice, and boxes arrive about 2 to 5 business days after the order bills, per the FAQ.
- **Sign-up offers.** On November 4, 2026, the home page offered a free protein for a year (or chicken for life) when you sign up. Read the terms, and count the box price without the free item when you compare.
- **Other ways to buy.** ButcherBox also runs a non-box store, The Market, which advertised free shipping on orders "as low as $50," but it listed no grass-fed beef when we looked. Separately, ButcherBox announced in January 2026 that five fresh grass-fed beef items, including ground beef and ribeye, sirloin and strip steaks, were on sale in more than 1,400 Target stores. If you buy in a store, check the origin line on the package.

## Freezer space

A full box is easy to fit. A bundle is not. Oklahoma State University Extension says to allow about 1 cubic foot of freezer space for every 35 to 40 pounds of cut and wrapped beef. By that rule, our estimate is that the Quarter-Cow Box (about 85 pounds) needs roughly 2 to 2.5 cubic feet and the Half-Cow Box (about 151 pounds) needs roughly 3.8 to 4.3 cubic feet. Measure your freezer before you order a bundle. See [quarter vs half vs whole cow](/guides/quarter-vs-half-vs-whole-cow/) for help sizing a freezer.

## Who it suits, and who it doesn't

ButcherBox's grass-fed line may suit you if:

- You want a low per-pound price on grass-fed ground beef delivered to your door.
- You are fine with beef raised in Australia.
- You like a subscription and the convenience of free shipping on every box.

Look elsewhere if:

- You want US-raised beef, or you want to know the specific farm or ranch.
- You want a named grass-fed certification on every product. ButcherBox names welfare certifiers, not a grass-fed one.
- You want to choose how your beef is cut, the way you can with a farm share.

## The bottom line

ButcherBox's 100% grass-fed beef is Australian, described by the company as fed on grass and forage for life, and sold mainly through a flexible subscription with free shipping. Its ground beef was the cheapest per pound of the six services in our delivery comparison, and on November 4, 2026, its bulk boxes came to roughly $9 to $10 a pound. Before you sign up, read the label on each item (grass-fed or grain-finished), decide whether imported beef is fine for you, and confirm the current price and offer terms on ButcherBox's site.
