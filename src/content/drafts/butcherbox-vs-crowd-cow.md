ButcherBox was the cheaper of the two on every grass-fed cut we could match on November 9, 2026: about $8 a pound for ground beef against about $15 at Crowd Cow. Crowd Cow costs more, but you can see a producer name on each listing and it has a few beef items from US suppliers, while ButcherBox says all its 100% grass-fed beef comes from Australia. Pick on what matters most to you: lowest price or knowing the supplier.

## How we compared them

Everything here comes from the two companies' own websites, read on November 9, 2026. We only compared beef each company tags as 100% grass-fed. We did not order, cook or taste anything from either company. Prices and offers change often, and both sites were running holiday promotions, so treat every number as a snapshot and confirm the price before you order.

## What "grass-fed" means at each company

Both companies also sell grain-finished beef, meaning the animal ate grain near the end of its life. So the first job at either store is to read the tag on each item.

**ButcherBox.** Its FAQ and help center say its grass-fed cattle eat grass and forage on pasture for their whole lives. The FAQ also calls this beef grass-finished, meaning the cattle stayed on grass and forage up to slaughter. The product pages for ground beef, burgers, ribeyes and New York strips say "100% grass-fed" and do not use the word grass-finished. ButcherBox says each product is labeled either 100% grass-fed or grain-finished, and its product list tags grass-fed cuts "100% Grass-Fed, Pasture-Raised." Some items on the same list are tagged grain-finished instead, so check each one.

**Crowd Cow.** Its main beef page calls its 100% Grass Fed line grass-fed and grass-finished. The same page describes its "Pasture Raised" beef as grain-finished. That catches shoppers out, because "pasture-raised" tells you where an animal spent time, not what it ate at the end. One product page shows how close the two can sit: the Premium Ground Beef page listed a Montana grain-finished offer and a Nebraska 100% grass-fed offer on the same page, at different prices. Check which offer is selected before you add it to your cart.

Our guide to [grass-fed vs grass-finished](/guides/grass-fed-vs-grass-finished/) explains why the finishing diet matters.

## Certifications

Neither company named a third-party grass-fed certifier on the pages we read. A certifier is an outside organization that checks a farm's practices against a written standard.

- **ButcherBox** says all its beef is certified pasture-raised and that its land-based suppliers are audited and certified by Certified Humane or Global Animal Partnership. Both are animal welfare programs. It also sells a 93/7 ground beef titled "Regenerative Organic Certified," but that product page names no certifier or level. A Regenerative Organic Certified Bronze logo appears on the home page.
- **Crowd Cow's** "100% Grass Fed" badge is its own label. "Certified Piedmontese" on some listings is a producer's name, not a certification body. A general note on its grass-fed pages mentions a regenerative program called Ecological Outcome Verified, but does not say any listing carries it.

Our [certifications page](/certifications/) lists the programs that check what cattle eat.

On antibiotics and hormones, ButcherBox says its proteins never have antibiotics or added hormones. Crowd Cow's FAQ says products are raised without "unnecessary antibiotics" or any added hormones. "Unnecessary" leaves room for treating sick animals, although a collection page says the Uruguayan cattle are raised without antibiotics or growth hormones.

## Where the cattle come from

**ButcherBox.** The help center says its grass-fed beef is from Australia, and the product list marks the ribeyes and New York strips "Product of Australia." The company says it prioritizes US sourcing overall, but its FAQ places US beef in the grain-finished line. So if you want US-raised grass-fed beef, ButcherBox's grass-fed range is not it.

**Crowd Cow.** Every listing shows a producer and location. On November 9, 2026, the available items badged 100% Grass Fed came from four suppliers:

| Supplier as listed | Location as listed | Available grass-fed items |
|---|---|---|
| Frigorifico Pando | Uruguay | Filet mignon, ribeye, ribeye pair, tri-tip, burger patties, stir fry |
| Certified Piedmontese | Lincoln, Nebraska | Ground beef, tenderloin medallions, New York strip |
| Thousand Hills Lifetime Grazed | Minnesota | Top sirloin |
| Pederson's Natural Beef Farms | Wisconsin | Hot dogs |

Six of those 11 listings were from Uruguay. Twelve more grass-fed listings were sold out, several of them Certified Piedmontese cuts and sausages, so the US range can be thin. A listed location is where Crowd Cow says the supplier is. If you need to know where the animal itself was raised, ask.

A few cautions:

- Fat levels and suppliers differ, so these are not identical products. The Crowd Cow New York strip is Certified Piedmontese, and a cheaper Uruguayan strip ($27.99 for 12 oz, about $37.32 a pound) was sold out.
- ButcherBox's ribeye page also showed a struck-through $35.00 price, so what you pay may differ from the $38.50 list price. Check at checkout.

- Crowd Cow's filet mignon was $58.98 a pound and its tri-tip $21.60 a pound. ButcherBox's product list showed these cuts without dollar prices, so we left them out of the table.

Crowd Cow sells by the item, so there is no box price to compare. Its bundles mix farms and were not tagged grass-fed, so check each item inside.

## Shipping and delivery

| | ButcherBox | Crowd Cow |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Free on subscription orders; shipping and handling may apply to one-time and à la carte orders | Free at $149 or more; $19.99 below that |
| Faster option | None stated on the pages we read | Priority shipping for $39.99 extra |
| Delivery time | FAQ: about 2 to 5 business days after billing. Home page: within 1 to 3 days | 1 to 2 business days after payment to most lower 48 ZIP codes |
| Alaska and Hawaii | We could not confirm | Does not ship there |
| Packing | Ships frozen | Ships frozen with dry ice |

The shipping rules change the real price. At Crowd Cow, 2 pounds of ground beef costs $29.98 plus $19.99 shipping, which comes to about $25 a pound. You reach free shipping at about 10 pounds of ground beef ($149.90). On ButcherBox, 10 pounds of ground beef at list price is about $80 to $82.50. The catch is that free shipping there applies to subscription boxes, and the smallest size in its FAQ is $179.

## How the subscriptions work

**ButcherBox.** A subscription is the main way to buy. You choose the box and the cuts, and most members get a box every 4 weeks, with 2 and 6 week options. You can pause or cancel any time from your Member Account page. Changes must be made by 11:59 p.m. Eastern the day before your bill date, and orders already being processed still ship. If you do not want a subscription, its help center lists gift, one-time and on-demand boxes plus an à la carte store, and says select cuts are sold at Target.

**Crowd Cow.** You can buy individual items, and recurring boxes are an option. Boxes come every 2, 4, 6 or 8 weeks, with no membership fee. Subscribers get the lowest listed price on each cut and extra rewards points. Crowd Cow builds the box from your preferences and its stock, then tells you before packing so you can swap items. You can skip or pause without penalty until packing starts. Two things to check:

- Its beef page FAQ says that, by default, your order will come monthly. Before you pay, confirm whether your cart is set to a one-time order or a recurring box.
- Its subscription and account help pages explain pausing and skipping, but we did not find steps for canceling outright. Look under My Account or ask support before you sign up. Because boxes are built partly from stock, also confirm every item carries the 100% Grass Fed badge.

## Which one should you choose?

ButcherBox probably fits better if:

- You mainly want the lowest per-pound price on grass-fed ground beef and everyday steaks.
- You are fine with beef from Australia.
- You will take a regular box of several proteins.

Crowd Cow probably fits better if:

- You want to buy a few cuts without committing to a box.
- You want to see a producer name on each item, or you want one of its few US grass-fed listings.

Neither fits well if you want a named grass-fed certification or a broad range of US-raised, grass-finished beef. For that, compare more services in our guide to [grass-fed beef delivery services](/guides/best-grass-fed-beef-delivery/), or buy from a farm.

## The bottom line

On November 9, 2026, ButcherBox had the lower prices and the free-shipping subscription, with all its grass-fed beef coming from Australia. Crowd Cow cost more, but it let you buy by the item and showed a producer on every listing, mostly from Uruguay. Whichever you choose, check the tag on each item, because both sell grain-finished beef too, and confirm the current price on the company's site.
