US Wellness Meats is a Missouri company that ships frozen beef, lamb, bison, cheese and other foods to all 50 states. It says its beef is "100% grass-fed and grass-finished," mostly from farms in northeast Missouri and west central Illinois. On November 11, 2026, its 25 pound ground beef bundles worked out to about $10 to $12 a pound on sale, and single steaks ran about $35 to $38 a pound. No outside grass-fed certifier is named for its beef, so you are relying on the company's own description.

## How we put this review together

Everything here comes from US Wellness Meats' own website and blog, read on November 11, 2026, plus the price we recorded for our [grass-fed beef delivery comparison](/guides/best-grass-fed-beef-delivery/) on October 28, 2026. We did not order, cook or taste anything. Prices and stock change often, so check the site before you buy.

## Who they are

US Wellness Meats also sells under the name Grassland Beef, and its website is grasslandbeef.com. Its address is in Canton, Missouri, and the site dates the business to 2000. The company says it is owned and run by family farms. The one person it names is John Wood, described as a fifth-generation cattle farmer and managing partner.

There is no subscription. You place one-off orders, and the company also runs a separate monthly shipment of fresh (never frozen) beef, which it says usually goes out on the third Wednesday of the month.

## Where the beef comes from

The company's farmers and partners page says most of its beef comes from the farms of three founding members in northeast Missouri and west central Illinois. It also lists beef farms in Alabama and Montana, and a small number of partner farms in Tasmania, Australia. Some Tasmanian cuts, such as skirt, flank and hanger steaks, are shipped by sea and then processed and packed in Missouri. If you want only US-raised beef, ask which farm a cut came from before you order those items.

The customer service page says animals are processed in small, USDA-inspected plants. Beef is wet-aged (aged in its vacuum bag) for about four weeks before freezing, according to the shipping FAQ.

## What "grass-finished" means here

"Grass-fed" on a label only promises that the animal ate grass at some point. "Grass-finished" means it kept eating grass and forage until slaughter, with no grain at the end. Our guide to [grass-fed vs grass-finished](/guides/grass-fed-vs-grass-finished/) explains the gap in detail.

US Wellness Meats states that its cattle get no grain or starch at any stage of life, and the customer service page applies the same rule to its lamb, bison and elk. For winter, the farmers page says the farms bale warm-season grasses in summer to feed when snow covers the pasture. The beef pages also say the cattle never go to a feedlot and get no antibiotics, added growth hormones or GMOs.

Not everything on the site is grass-fed, and the company says so:

| Product | What the company says about feed | Third-party label named |
|---|---|---|
| Beef | 100% grass-fed and grass-finished, no grain | None |
| Lamb, bison | 100% grass-fed, no grain | None |
| Pork | Vegetarian, non-GMO feed that includes corn and soy | "GAP-certified" (no level given) |
| Chicken, turkey | Pasture-raised, plus non-GMO corn and soy feed | None |
| Cheese | Milk from 100% grass-fed cows, no grain | None (not certified organic) |

The company explains that pigs have a single stomach and can't be raised on grass alone, which is why its pork gets grain. Just don't assume every item in your box is grass-finished.

## Certifications

The company says it follows organic principles but is not certified organic. None of the pages we read names a grass-fed certifier such as the American Grassfed Association for its beef. The only certifications mentioned are a "GAP-certified" claim for its pork, with no level or certificate number, and an organic label on one ghee product. See our [certifications page](/certifications/) for what each program checks.

That doesn't mean the claims are false. It means no outside auditor is named, so you are trusting the company's word. If a certified label matters to you, compare with farms in our [directory](/farms/) that list one.

## Cheese and butter

US Wellness Meats sells eleven cheeses, including cheddar, Colby, Gouda, havarti, Monterey jack and Swiss. On November 11, 2026, each 8 ounce block listed at $12.82, or about $25.64 a pound. A separate mozzarella page listed a 10.5 ounce piece at $14.95, so check which size you are buying.

The company calls its cheese raw, meaning made from milk that was not pasteurized (heated to kill bacteria). The Colby page lists the ingredients as raw whole milk, sea salt, animal rennet and cultures, and gives a highest heat of 107°F. The customer service page says cheese is heated to no more than 102°F (107°F for Swiss). By comparison, it says its butter is pasteurized at 161.6°F for 15 seconds. So the cheese is raw by the company's definition and the butter is not. The product pages call the cheese "aged" but don't give an aging time. The company's raw cheese blog post says federal rules require raw-milk cheese to be aged at least 60 days. Our [raw milk laws guide](/guides/raw-milk-laws-by-state/) covers how rules differ for fluid milk.

Where the cheese comes from is not fully consistent across the site. The farmers page names two Amish dairies, one in Indiana and one in Pennsylvania. A blog post and the product photos name Lykens Valley Creamery as the cheese maker. The product pages describe the milk as A2/A2 (from cows that produce one type of milk protein) and say the cows get no grain.

We couldn't load the butter page, so we can't report its price or size. The dairy page describes the butter as non-homogenized and minimally pasteurized.

## How shipping works

| Detail | What the site says |
|---|---|
| Carrier | FedEx only, Ground or Express depending on your ZIP code |
| Ship days | Monday to Wednesday (orders after 10 a.m. Central on Wednesday ship the next Monday) |
| Delivery | Usually 1 to 3 business days after shipping |
| Free shipping | Continental US orders of 15 lb or more |
| Smaller orders | No minimum; the shipping FAQ lists a $15 fee under 15 lb |
| Small-order warning | Orders under 7 lb may not stay frozen and are not covered by the satisfaction guarantee |
| Alaska and Hawaii | $35 flat, 20 to 35 lb, per the main site (an older FAQ says $20) |
| Puerto Rico | $120 flat, 20 to 35 lb, per the main site (an older FAQ says $85) |
| Packing | Vacuum-sealed and frozen, in insulated coolers; most coolers are recyclable |

Cheese ships cold but not frozen, according to the Gouda page. The company doesn't ship to Canada. Some items, such as certain chicken bundles, ship separately from the farm and don't count toward the 15 pound free-shipping weight.

## Who it suits, and who it doesn't

US Wellness Meats may suit you if:

- You want beef described as grass-finished and mostly raised in the US, and you can't find a local farm.
- You want to buy ground beef in 25 pound lots, or whole primals to cut yourself.
- You'd like raw-milk cheese shipped to your door alongside meat.
- You'd rather place one-off orders than sign up for a subscription.

It may not suit you if:

- You want a third-party certified grass-fed label. None is named for the beef.
- You want to know the exact farm every cut came from. Ask first, since some cuts come from Tasmania.
- You order in small amounts. Under 15 pounds you pay for shipping, and under 7 pounds the guarantee doesn't apply.
- You want the lowest price on steaks. Single cut steaks ran $35 or more a pound on the day we looked.

## Before you order

1. Add up the weight. Aim for at least 15 pounds to get free shipping in the continental US, and never go under 7.
2. Check each item's page for "sold out" and for wording on origin if US-raised matters to you.
3. Make sure you have freezer space ready before a large order arrives.
4. Order before Wednesday at 10 a.m. Central if you want your box to ship that week; later orders wait until the next Monday.
5. Confirm the current shipping fee for Alaska, Hawaii or Puerto Rico with customer service, since the site gives two different figures.

## The bottom line

US Wellness Meats says all its beef is grass-finished with no grain at any stage, and most of it is raised in Missouri and Illinois. Its 25 pound ground beef bundles were among the lower per-pound prices for grass-finished beef in our [delivery comparison](/guides/best-grass-fed-beef-delivery/). The trade-offs are no named grass-fed certifier, some imported cuts, and high prices for single steaks. Read each product page, order in bulk to make shipping worth it, and confirm current prices with the company.
